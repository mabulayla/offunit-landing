Dating for firefighters

A dating app for firefighters who work 24s, 48s and every other holiday

Most dating apps assume your week runs Monday to Friday. Yours runs in 24-hour blocks, and some of them land on Christmas. OFFUNIT is a free dating app for firefighters and other people who work shifts, so the schedule is something your matches already expect.

Why dating is harder on a firefighter's schedule

The job itself isn't the problem. The rotation is, in ways people outside the fire service don't see:

  • Your days off don't match anyone's weekend. On a 24-on/48-off or a 48/96 you might have four days free midweek and then spend all of Saturday at the station.
  • Holidays and birthdays happen without you. Thanksgiving, Christmas morning, their best friend's birthday dinner. Someone has to be on, and some years it's your shift.
  • On shift, the tones come first. You can't promise to text back at 2pm, and "dinner after work" doesn't exist on a shift day.
  • The morning after isn't always a day off. A 24 with runs all night means your first day off starts with recovery, not brunch.
  • Wildland season takes you away. Crews can be out for weeks on an assignment with patchy signal. That's a lot to explain to someone you've been on three dates with.
  • The station is a second family. You cook, train and sleep alongside your crew. Anyone you date is getting to know them too.

None of that means you can't date someone with a 9-to-5. It just means more explaining up front, and the first month goes easier with someone who expects it.

Dating advice that fits the job

Put your rotation up front

Mention early that you work 24s and what your rotation looks like. If someone hears "I'm at the station every third day" and gets excited about the long stretches off, that's a good sign.

Use the long stretches

A 48/96 gives you four days in a row. That's room for a day trip, a midweek getaway, or a couple of real dates in one week. Plan around those blocks instead of squeezing something into the morning you get off.

Share the calendar

Your rotation is set well ahead, Kelly days included. Once you're seeing someone regularly, send them the next couple of months. It saves a lot of "are you free Friday?" and shows you're planning to be around.

Talk about the worry

A partner who worries every shift is normal. You can't make the risk go away, but you can make the waiting easier. Explain that quiet on a shift day usually just means you're busy, and agree on a quick text when you're back in quarters or off in the morning.

Pick your stories

The calls you laugh about at the kitchen table can land hard on a first date. Save the heavy ones for someone who knows you, and have a few things to talk about that aren't work.

Introduce them to the crew

Sooner or later they'll meet the station family, at a cookout or someone's kid's birthday. Give them a heads up on who's who. It's a lot of nicknames and inside jokes at once.

Get ahead of wildland season

If you go out on fires, have the honest talk before season starts: how long you might be gone, how often you'll have signal, and that plans made in June may not survive August. Saying it early is what keeps it from turning into a fight later.

Dating someone else who works shifts

Plenty of firefighters end up with nurses, medics, dispatchers and cops. Part of that is who you run into on the job. Part of it is that they don't need the schedule explained. They've worked holidays too, and an 0800 breakfast date right after shift change doesn't seem strange to them.

The tradeoff is two rotations to line up instead of one. Compare calendars early, and treat the days you're both off as the ones worth protecting.
