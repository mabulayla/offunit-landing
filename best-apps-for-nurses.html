Apps for nurses

The best apps for nurses, from drug lookups to meeting people

The best apps for nurses are the ones that solve one real problem: a quick drug lookup, an extra shift, a study plan, a budget, or someone to talk to who gets your schedule. Here's an honest roundup, with what each is for.

Clinical reference: look it up, don't guess

These are the apps most likely to earn a spot on your home screen. None of them replace your hospital's policies or the pharmacist, but they're quick for the 3am "wait, what's the max dose?" moment.

Medscape

A free drug reference with an interaction checker, plus disease references and news. Good as a first stop for drug lookups.

Epocrates

Concise drug monographs and an interaction checker. There's a free tier and a paid Plus tier with more content. Try the free one first and see if you miss anything.

Always check your facility's approved references too. What your unit trusts matters more than which app is prettier.

Picking up extra shifts

Nursa

A per diem app where CNAs, LPNs, RNs and some allied health clinicians browse open shifts at nearby facilities and pick up as many or as few as they want. It isn't in every state, and app store reviews are mixed, so look at what's actually posted near you before counting on it.

Before you sign up for any shift app, ask about how and when you get paid, cancellation rules, and whether the facility will orient you. A great rate on a unit you've never seen is a different job than your home unit.

Studying: NCLEX and certifications

If you're a student or new grad, a question bank is the usual route.

Archer Review

Often described as the budget-friendly option, with adaptive practice exams. There's a free trial in the app before you pay.

UWorld

Often described as the pricier one, praised for detailed rationales. Compare current prices before choosing.

Nursing.com

Video lessons and study tools, with a free NCLEX app.

Pick one and stick with it. Switching between three banks mostly burns money and time.

Money: the 12-hour-shift problem

Shift pay is lumpy. Differentials, overtime, and a travel contract with a gap between assignments all make a normal monthly budget awkward.

YNAB

A paid budgeting app (You Need A Budget) with a free trial. It works by giving every dollar a job, which suits irregular paychecks. Check current pricing on their site.

Whatever you use, the useful habit is the same: budget from your lowest normal paycheck, and treat extra shifts as bonus money.

How to choose without cluttering your phone

  • Start with the problem. Need a drug lookup, a shift, a study plan, a budget, or people? One app per problem is plenty.
  • Try free before paying. Most of these have a free tier or a trial.
  • Check the date. Pricing and features change. Confirm on the official site, not a review from three years ago.
  • Keep patient information out. Reference apps are for looking things up. Follow your employer's policy on phones and never share patient details in personal apps.
  • Delete what you don't open. If it hasn't been used in a month, it's just taking space.

Why the list is short

There are hundreds of nursing apps, and most are variations on the same few ideas. This list sticks to ones that exist, do what they say, and cover a different need. If you've found one that belongs here and isn't listed, it's probably because we haven't tried it, not because it's bad.

Prices and features here are general on purpose. Check each official site for what's current.

Questions people ask

What are the best apps for nurses?

It depends on the job. For clinical lookups, a drug reference like Medscape or Epocrates. For extra shifts, a per diem app like Nursa. For NCLEX prep, a question bank like Archer Review or UWorld. For budgeting, something like YNAB. For meeting other healthcare workers, OFFUNIT.

Are there free apps for nurses?

Yes. Medscape's drug reference is free, Epocrates has a free tier, and Nursa is free for clinicians to sign up. Question banks and budgeting apps like YNAB usually cost money after a trial, so check current pricing first.

What app can nurses use to pick up extra shifts?

Per diem apps like Nursa let licensed clinicians browse open shifts at nearby facilities. Availability depends on your state and license type, so look at what's open near you before relying on it.

Is there an app for nurses to meet other nurses?

OFFUNIT is a free social app for people who work shifts in nursing, healthcare and emergency services, with Dating, Friends and Networking modes on one profile. It's new, so some cities are busier than others.

Can I use my phone for patient information at work?

Follow your employer's policy. Don't put patient information into personal apps, texts or social media. Clinical reference apps are for looking things up, not for storing patient details.
