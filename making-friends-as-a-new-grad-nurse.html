Friends for new grad nurses

Making friends as a new grad nurse when you moved for the job

You passed the NCLEX, signed the offer, packed the car, and now you're in a city where you know your preceptor and a barista. Making friends as a new grad nurse is its own project, and nobody warned you about it in school.

Why it's harder than it sounds

In college, friends happened by default. Now you're doing it on purpose, on a schedule that fights you.

  • Your first year is mostly orientation, learning and sleep. There's not much energy left for a social life you haven't built yet.
  • Your schedule doesn't match anyone's. Weekday trivia night and Saturday brunch both assume you're free. You might be working both.
  • Your old friends are on a different clock. They're a text away but a time zone and a shift pattern apart.
  • You're tired in a way that makes cancelling feel like relief. That's normal. It's also how weeks go by without talking to anyone.

Start with the people you already see

Your cohort

Many hospitals run a new grad or nurse residency program, usually about a year long, where you meet regularly with other new nurses. Those people are in the exact same spot you are. Get the group chat going and suggest a coffee after the next class. Most of them are hoping someone else will.

Your preceptor and unit

Coworkers can become real friends, and they understand the job. Say yes to the unit potluck. Ask someone what they do on days off. Small, low-pressure invitations beat waiting to be included.

One rule

Keep at least a couple of friends outside your unit. When work is rough, it helps to have someone who hasn't heard every detail of it.

Build a routine outside work

Friendships come from seeing the same people repeatedly. So pick things that repeat:

  • A class or a gym. Look for ones with several time slots a week, so a rotating schedule doesn't lock you out.
  • A rec league or run club. Casual ones are easy to drop into. Tell the organizer you work shifts and may miss weeks.
  • A regular spot. A coffee shop, a climbing gym, a dog park. Familiar faces turn into hellos, and hellos turn into plans.
  • Volunteering. Shift-friendly options exist, and you meet people who showed up on purpose.

Pick one thing, not five. A routine you actually keep beats an ambitious plan you drop in week two.

Making plans on a shift schedule

  • Suggest a specific time. "Want to get food Thursday at 2?" works better than "we should hang out sometime."
  • Share your schedule early. Once the schedule is posted, tell new friends your next few weeks. It saves ten back-and-forth texts.
  • Use post-shift windows. Breakfast after a night shift, or an early dinner before a late one, can work well if you aren't wiped out.
  • Expect to be the one who initiates for a while. Everyone is busy, and the person who sends the second message often ends up with the friends.
  • Cancel kindly and reschedule right away. A call-in or a shift that runs long happens. Offering a new time in the same message keeps the friendship alive.

If it feels heavy

Loneliness in a first year is common, and it usually eases as you build a routine. But if you've felt low for weeks, aren't sleeping well even on off days, or dread work in a way that scares you, tell someone: a doctor, a counselor, or your employer's assistance program if it has one. Asking for help early doesn't make you a weak nurse.

Questions people ask

How do I make friends as a new grad nurse in a new city?

Start with the people already around you: your residency cohort, your preceptor and your unit. Then add one or two things outside work on a regular schedule, like a class, a gym or a club, so you see the same faces. Apps with a friends mode, including OFFUNIT, can help you meet other shift workers nearby.

Is it normal to feel lonely during your first year as a nurse?

Yes. A new job, a new city and a changing schedule all hit at once, and a lot of new grads feel isolated for a while. If it starts to affect your sleep or mood for weeks at a time, talk to a doctor or counselor.

Should I be friends with my coworkers?

It's fine and often helpful, since they get the job. Just keep some friends outside your unit so a bad week at work doesn't take your whole social life with it.

Can I use OFFUNIT just to find friends?

Do I need to be a nurse or verified to use OFFUNIT?
