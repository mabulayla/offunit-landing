Dating for travel nurses

Dating as a travel nurse, when every relationship starts with an end date

Dating as a travel nurse has one problem regular dating doesn't: you know roughly when you're leaving. That doesn't make it impossible. It just means being more honest, earlier, than most people are used to.

Why it's different for travelers

Many travel contracts run about 13 weeks, though they can be shorter or longer, and extensions happen when both you and the facility want one. In that time you're learning a new unit, a new city and a new commute, often on nights or a schedule you didn't pick.

  • There's a clock. Both of you know it's running, even if nobody says so.
  • The first few weeks are busy. Housing, orientation and figuring out the unit eat the energy you'd normally put into meeting people.
  • Your days off don't match anyone's. Three 12s with random days off is hard to plan around for someone with a 9-to-5.
  • You don't have a local network. No friends to set you up, and no one to tell you which bar is actually good.

Be upfront about the timeline

Say you're a traveler, and roughly when your contract ends, early. Before or on the first date is ideal. Something like:

"Heads up, I'm a travel nurse. I'm here until about mid-March, and I'm not sure yet where I'm going next."

That one line lets the other person decide what they're okay with. Some people are happy to see where a few months goes. Some only want something long term and will bow out, which is better to learn on day one than week ten. Leaving it out tends to turn a good thing into a hard conversation later.

Decide what you want this contract

Both of these are fine, as long as you're honest with yourself and the other person:

  • Something casual. Company for a few months in a new city, with no plan past the contract. Say so plainly.
  • Something that could last. If you're open to extending, coming back, or settling down somewhere, that changes who you should be looking for and how you talk about the future.

It's normal for the answer to change partway through an assignment. If it does, say that too.

Dating someone on your unit

Coworkers are the people you see most on a new assignment, so it happens. A few things are worth thinking about first:

  • Check the rules. Facilities and agencies may have policies about relationships at work, especially with anyone in your chain of command.
  • Units talk. As the new person, you're already noticed. A relationship on the unit will be common knowledge quickly.
  • You leave, they stay. If it ends badly, you're gone in a few weeks and they're still working with everyone who watched it happen. Be kind about that.

When it turns into something more

Sometimes a contract romance becomes a real relationship. Have the "what now?" talk a few weeks before the contract ends, not on the last day. The usual options:

  • Extend. If the facility offers it and you want it, an extension buys time.
  • Stay in the area. Look for your next contract nearby, or take a staff job.
  • Go long distance. Plenty of travelers do this between assignments.
  • Travel together. If they can work remotely, or are a traveler too, you may be able to pick assignments in the same area. That isn't always possible, so have a backup plan.

Making long distance work between contracts

  • Use your schedule. Stacking your three 12s back to back can free up four or more days in a row for a visit, if your unit allows it.
  • Always have the next visit booked. Distance is easier when there's a date on the calendar.
  • Share calendars. Your shifts, their commitments, time zones. It cuts down on missed calls.
  • Talk about the end of the distance. Long distance works better when you both know roughly when and how it ends.

Questions people ask

Should I tell a date I'm a travel nurse on a contract?

Yes, early. Mention that you're a traveler and roughly when your contract ends before or on the first date. It lets the other person decide what they're comfortable with and avoids a hard conversation later.

Can a relationship work if one person is a travel nurse?

Yes. Couples make it work by extending a contract, taking the next assignment nearby, going long distance for a while, or traveling together. The key is talking about it a few weeks before the contract ends, not on the last day.

Is it okay for a travel nurse to date a coworker?

It happens, but check your facility's and agency's policies first, especially about anyone you report to. Keep in mind that units talk, and that you'll leave while they stay.

How long are travel nurse contracts?

Many travel nursing contracts run about 13 weeks, though they can be shorter or longer, and extensions happen when both you and the facility agree. Check your own contract for the details.
