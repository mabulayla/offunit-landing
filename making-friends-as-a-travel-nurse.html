Friends for nurses

Making friends as a travel nurse, when you start over every 13 weeks

Making friends as a travel nurse has a built-in problem: just when the city starts to feel familiar, the contract is up. Here's how to build a social life quickly, and without burning out on small talk.

Why it's harder for travelers

Many travel contracts run 13 weeks, though they can be shorter or longer, and extensions happen when both sides agree. That's roughly three months to learn a new unit, a new charting system, a new commute and a new city.

  • You arrive in the middle of everyone else's life. Staff nurses have friends, families and routines. You're friendly, but you're temporary, and everyone knows it.
  • Your schedule is odd. Nights, three 12s, a stretch of days off that don't line up with anyone's weekend.
  • The first month is all logistics. Housing, orientation, finding groceries. Social life gets pushed to "later," and later is week nine.
  • It's easy to hide in the apartment. Tired after a 12, new place, nobody expecting you anywhere. A week turns into six.

What actually works

Say yes in the first two weeks

Invitations dry up fast if you decline the first few. Early on, accept the lunch, the post-shift food run, the "a few of us are going to..." Even if you leave after an hour.

Pick one thing that repeats

One-off meetups rarely turn into friendships. A weekly class, run club, climbing gym, trivia night or church group puts the same faces in front of you again and again. Choose something that fits your days off and go for the whole contract.

Work friends count

Coworkers are your fastest route to people. Ask where they eat, what they do on their days off, what you shouldn't miss. Most staff nurses like being asked, and it makes you the person who's easy to invite.

Look for other travelers

Other travelers have the same clock you do. They know the contract is short, so there's no awkward "should I invest in this?" The hospital break room, traveler groups online and your agency's social events are all places to start.

Plan around your own energy

You don't need a packed calendar. Two or three people you actually like beats ten you're performing for. Protect your sleep, and be honest when you need a quiet day.

Stay in touch with the last city

A group chat with the friends from your last assignment makes the first lonely week in the new place easier, and some of them will turn up in your next city.

A simple first-month plan

  • Week one: learn the unit, and ask two coworkers where they like to eat and what you should do on your days off.
  • Week two: pick your repeating thing (a class, a club, a weekly game night) and go to it once.
  • Week three: go back to it. Invite one person from work to something low-key, like coffee or a walk.
  • Week four: make one plan with someone you've met twice, and put it on the calendar before your next block of shifts starts.

It's not complicated. The hard part is doing it while you're tired, in a new place, with a schedule that's still settling. A short list you can follow without thinking helps more than a big plan.

Questions people ask

How do travel nurses make friends in a new city?

Start with the people around you at work, then add things that repeat every week: a gym class, a run club, a regular spot. Use your first two weeks to say yes to most invitations, and use friends-focused apps or local groups to find other travelers. OFFUNIT has a Friends mode for this, with shift type on profiles.

How long is a typical travel nurse contract?

Many travel nursing contracts run 13 weeks, though they can be shorter or longer, and extensions are common when both you and the facility agree. Your own contract is what counts, so check it.

Is it worth making friends if I'm only there 13 weeks?

Usually yes. Even a few people to eat dinner with or hike with change how a contract feels. Some friendships last past the assignment, and travelers you meet often turn up in your next city.
